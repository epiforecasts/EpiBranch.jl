Writing an intervention ​

This page is for a control measure the package does not include: a treatment that ends infections early, a vaccination campaign aimed at a particular group, a rule for whose contacts are traced, a policy that starts on a given date or is limited by the doses available. It also covers clinical events of your own, such as testing, treatment or loss to follow-up, which use the same hooks. It builds on the border closure in Extending EpiBranch, which used a single method; here are all the steps of a simulation an intervention can act at, and what each is for. The words used in these pages are defined in that guide.

An intervention is a struct that subtypes AbstractIntervention. You write methods only for the hooks it needs; every other hook leaves the simulation unchanged.

What happens to each case ​

On a branching process the simulation works one generation at a time. In each generation:

  1. The transmission model gives each case of the previous generation its potential secondary cases, its contacts, each with a potential infection time. Each new person is created, and initialise_individual! sets the values the intervention keeps for them (ContactTracing sets :traced to false).

  2. The cases are then taken one at a time, in order of infection time. Every intervention's trace_contacts! receives the contacts that case made; contact tracing happens here. Every intervention's intervention_actions then proposes actions on those contacts, such as doses of a vaccine, which go ahead only if their schedule and capacity limits allow. Last, keep_active can keep contacts who end up not infected in the simulation, so that their own contacts are generated too (needed for tracing contacts of contacts).

  3. For each pair of infector and contact, competing_risk says whether anything stops the transmission. The contact is infected only if nothing does.

  4. Each newly infected contact is settled: its clinical transitions run (onset, reporting, outcome), and then every intervention's resolve_individual!. This is where Isolation works out when a case is isolated, from its onset time. These cases make the contacts of the next generation.

The index cases are settled in the same way before the first generation.

The network, household, homogeneous and mixing models (the continuous-time models) have no generations: they settle one case at a time, in order of infection time. Once a case's infection time is fixed, on_infection_settled! runs, then its clinical transitions and resolve_individual!. On a model that can name a case's contacts (a network or household model, not the homogeneous or mixing one), trace_contacts! and keep_active then act on the case's contacts not yet infected, and intervention_actions on the case and everyone not yet infected; ring and group vaccination narrow that to the people they reach. The case then makes its potential infections, and competing_risk is asked about each when the simulation reaches it in order of infection time, with the state as it stands then: a dose or quarantine recorded on the contact since the infection time was drawn is in force.

While a case's clinical transitions and intervention hooks run, from resolve_individual! on, state.max_infection_time is that case's infection time and state.cumulative_cases counts the case and those settled before it. on_infection_settled! runs before that, and still sees the previous case's values. A Scheduled condition reads these: cumulative_cases for a start_after_cases count. start_time/end_time are checked differently, against isolation_time/intervention_time rather than this clock; see below. On a branching process the count includes the cases of earlier generations and those of the same generation infected no later than the case; a case of a later generation is not counted even if it was infected earlier, because it has not been drawn yet.

Interventions are applied in the order they appear in interventions = [...], and each sees what earlier ones wrote for the same case.

A Risk is what competing_risk returns. Risk(event_time = t, block_probability = p) blocks a transmission happening at or after time t with probability p. An optional release_time ends the block, for a removal such as quarantine that ends. Return nothing when the intervention does not affect a pair, or a tuple of risks when it acts in more than one way: RingVaccination returns a risk on the contact (lower susceptibility) alongside one on the infector (lower onward infectiousness). A contact is infected only if no risk blocks it.

A measure that changes how many people a case infects, such as a cap on gathering size, belongs in the offspring distribution instead; see Change how many people each case infects.

reads_population_state covers everything an intervention hands work to: a component or function you supply counts towards its owner's answer. A built-in intervention asks its own components (ContactTracing its eligibility, rate, delay and action; Isolation its eligibility), each answering false by default. An eligibility, rate, delay or action of yours that tests a running case count, or anything else beyond the person being considered, declares true for itself, and its owner then answers true too. A plain function passed as a parameter has nowhere to declare this, and such a test belongs in a component type.

analytic_offspring_effect is for the rare intervention whose effect on the offspring distribution is exact in closed form. One that cuts every case's R by a fixed proportion p turns NegBin(R, k) into NegBin((1 - p) * R, k), and its method returns that; return a Poisson, a negative binomial, or a GeneratingFunctionOffspring built from the effect's own generating function, the forms the analytical functions accept. MassVaccination has such a method for a campaign already complete before the simulation starts. Most interventions still have no such form: contact tracing's and ring and group vaccination's effects depend on who they reach and when, and have no method. Isolation's effect on R depends on how its timing overlaps the generation time, which is a mixture over the isolation time rather than a fixed proportion; it defines the three-argument form to build that mixture from the model's generation time and incubation period, and its own docstring sets out the limits of this (a custom eligibility rule, say, still has no closed form). extinction_probability, epidemic_probability, probability_contain and the other analytical functions stop with an error on a ModelSpec that holds an intervention with no method of either arity, and their error points to simulating the model instead. The border closure from Extending EpiBranch is one of these.

apply_post_transmission! is deprecated

Older interventions acted on a whole generation's contacts at once through apply_post_transmission!. Branching processes still call a method of it, with a deprecation warning, and the continuous-time models never do. Write what it did to each contact as trace_contacts!, and any action with a delivery date as intervention_actions.

The AbstractIntervention docstring and the Extension reference have the remaining details for the continuous-time models.

Which hooks run on which model ​

Not every hook is called by every model. A branching process creates a new Individual for every contact, infected or not. The continuous-time models (network, household, homogeneous, mixing) create everyone at the start and only work out when each person is infected, from draws of the contact interval. What they do have is each potential infection, a drawn time for a named pair, and a Risk can act on that, as can the end of the infectious period.

In practice:

  • An intervention that removes a case from transmission (isolation, quarantine once traced, hospitalisation) works on every model, because it shortens the infectious period, which every model has.

  • An intervention that blocks each contact with some probability (a leaky vaccine, partially effective prophylaxis) also works everywhere, and so do per-person susceptibility and infectiousness. The continuous-time models put each potential infection to the risks when they reach it, in order of infection time, at the time drawn for it. A blocked contact does not transmit, and the pair keeps meeting: the pair's next contact is drawn later.

  • SourceTracing quarantines whichever case it finds, so it is a removal too, and reaches every continuous-time model, including the mass-action pools ContactTracing cannot: a case's infector is read off the transmission tree rather than the model's own contact structure. On a branching process its secondary cases are already drawn by the time it can itself be found and traced back, so there SourceTracing only records the link.

The same efficacy means different things on the two kinds of model

On a continuous-time model a pair that escapes infection keeps meeting, so blocking each contact with probability p multiplies the rate of infection by 1 - p. A pair with a one-day mean contact interval and a two-day infectious period then infects 1 - exp(-1) = 0.63 of the time at efficacy 0.5. On a branching process a case's contacts are a fixed set of draws, so a blocked contact is a transmission lost, and the same efficacy of 0.5 halves that pair's chance of transmission (0.43 in this example, against 0.63). The per-exposure reading on continuous-time models is what a leaky vaccine means there; check which one your scenario needs.

  • On a network or household model, loglikelihood(data, spec) refuses any part of spec the likelihood cannot represent: a per-contact block that does not come from a recorded removal stretch (RingVaccination's onward_efficacy, say), or an effect not declared through infection_likelihood_compatible or susceptibility_components. Complete and leaky isolation are both fitted exactly (the latter through EpiBranch.removal_block_probability), and so is vaccination within the limits listed in Likelihood compatibility, when the fit is given the same spec as the simulation. Fitting with the bare model, or with a spec that leaves the intervention out, gives no error and disagrees with the simulation.

  • An intervention's actions reach the continuous-time models through one of two declarations. EpiBranch.continuous_actions(iv) = true offers them once a case settles, narrowed to the case and the contacts it can still reach; such actions must work without knowing a contact's infection time, which is not yet drawn, and must not change a case already settled. That narrowing needs a model that can name contacts, unless the intervention also declares EpiBranch.needs_contacts(iv) = false, as group vaccination does, since its candidates come from a case's group rather than its contacts. Ring vaccination declares continuous_actions too: it admits a dose for a contact not yet exposed as though any eligibility_window were open, and withdraws it through EpiBranch.on_infection_settled! once that contact's own infection settles and the window turns out not to cover it. EpiBranch.acts_on_population(iv) = true instead offers the actions once, before any case settles, to every member; MassVaccination declares it, since its rollout depends on nothing about who infected whom. The simulation names an intervention that declares neither in a warning, with the hooks it skips, and an intervention of your own needs no declaration for this.

  • Contact tracing needs both: its action is a removal, so it applies on every model, but it has to know who a case's contacts were. A branching process knows the contacts each case made. A continuous-time model must supply them: the model reports EpiBranch.supplies_contacts(model) = true and passes a contacts function. That function returns contact ids, or (id, time) pairs when some contacts arise only after the case's infection, such as at a funeral (contacts_from = :died on a RoutedNetwork route). time is when each person became a contact, and ContactTracing starts that contact's trace delay no earlier than then. A network names a person's neighbours and a household its members; the homogeneous and mixing models have no list of who met whom, so tracing does not happen there.

Order of steps on the two kinds of model ​

On both kinds of model a contact is traced when its infector is settled, before the contact's own clinical transitions and resolve_individual! run. An intervention that writes onto a contact must therefore not assume the contact has nothing written yet, and one that reads a contact's own values must not assume they are already set. On the continuous-time models even the contact's infection time and onset are not yet known. Isolation therefore treats a quarantine already in place as a competing route to isolation and keeps the earlier time.

On the continuous-time models tracing reaches only contacts whose own infection is not yet settled. Once a case is settled, its infectious period and onward transmissions are fixed, so a trace arriving afterwards has nothing left to shorten. Backward tracing, to the person who infected a case, is not supported on either kind of model.

What an intervention can rely on:

  • resolve_individual! runs on a case before it infects anyone, so a risk can read what resolve_individual! wrote on the infector.

  • A case's trace_contacts!, intervention_actions and keep_active run before any of its contacts is known to be infected or not, so a risk can read what they wrote on the contact (for example :vaccination_time).

  • Every intervention's trace_contacts! for a case runs before any intervention's intervention_actions for it, so ring vaccination reads what tracing wrote, whatever the order of the list.

  • Otherwise interventions apply in the order of interventions = [...], and each sees what earlier interventions wrote for the same case.

  • keep_active runs before its contacts are known to be infected, so choose by what tracing wrote, as ContactTracing does, not by :infected. A contact it keeps that is then infected goes on as a case anyway.

On the continuous-time models, the transmission time a Risk is compared with is the potential infection time drawn for that pair.

The hooks in the built-in interventions ​

These simplified versions of the package source show one hook each. They call internal functions and do not run on their own. The full source is in src/interventions/.

initialise_individual!: ContactTracing sets the two values it keeps on every new person, where code reading them later always finds a value.

julia
function initialise_individual!(::ContactTracing, individual, state)
    individual.state[:traced] = false
    individual.state[:quarantined] = false
    return nothing
end

resolve_individual!: Isolation works out when the case is isolated, from its onset time plus a sampled delay, and takes an earlier isolation time if contact tracing found the case first. It then draws how long the isolation lasts from its duration and passes the end to set_isolated! as release_time. x || return nothing means "stop here unless x is true".

julia
function resolve_individual!(iso::Isolation, individual, state)
    onset = onset_time(individual)
    # Self-isolation after a positive test.
    test_time = if is_test_positive(individual)
        onset + _sample_value(iso.onset_to_isolation_delay, state.rng, individual)
    else
        Inf
    end
    # A contact traced before its onset was known has only the bare trace
    # time, so hold it back to the onset.
    traced_time = max(get(individual.state, :_traced_isolation_time, Inf), onset)
    start = min(test_time, traced_time)
    isfinite(start) || return nothing
    duration = _removal_duration(
        iso.duration, state.rng, individual, "`Isolation`'s `duration`"
    )
    set_isolated!(individual, start; release_time = start + duration)
    return nothing
end

trace_contacts!: ContactTracing goes through the contacts a case reached and applies its trace action (Quarantine or FlagOnly) when the contact is eligible and is reached. The trace is timed from trigger_time, which for a policy based on isolation is the recorded isolation, so an isolation that does not count as a detection starts no trace. Leaving out the depth of the ring and the contacts' not_before times, it reads:

julia
function trace_contacts!(ct::ContactTracing, state, infector, contacts)
    rng = state.rng
    for ind in contacts
        is_eligible(ct.eligibility, infector, ind, state) || continue
        traces(ct.trace_rate, infector, ind, state, rng) || continue
        trace_delay = draw_trace_delay(ct.isolation_to_trace_delay, infector, ind, state, rng)
        trace_time = trigger_time(ct.eligibility, infector, ind, state) + trace_delay
        apply_trace!(ct.action, ind, state, trace_time, rng)
    end
    return nothing
end

competing_risk: see A custom intervention: closing a border.

Checking your intervention ​

Every hook has a default, so an intervention defines only the hooks it needs. Building a ModelSpec warns, naming the hook where it can, about a method written with a number of arguments no call uses, and about one written without the EpiBranch. prefix, which defines a new function of the same name instead of a method of the package's own. An intervention with no hook method at all gets a warning listing every hook. One that acts through something the check does not read, such as a function of your own package, defines EpiBranch.has_effect(::MyIntervention) = true to silence it. The warning appears once for each intervention type and set of methods.

None of this can tell an intervention that leaves a hook out on purpose from one missing a hook it needs. Three checks for the rest:

  • Run a small simulation (max_cases = 50) with and without your intervention. If the results look the same, your competing_risk or trace_contacts! is probably not being called for the people you expect.

  • Declare the values your intervention needs with required_fields (see Requiring values on each person), so the simulation stops with an error at the start when nothing sets them.

  • After a small run, look at state.individuals[1].state to confirm your hook wrote the values that later steps read.

Combining with built-in interventions ​

A custom intervention combines with the built-in ones. Here the border closure from Extending EpiBranch is added to isolation of symptomatic cases. Each case infects a number of people drawn from NegBin(2.5, 0.16) (mean R = 2.5, dispersion k = 0.16), the generation time is Exponential(5.0) (mean 5 days), the incubation period is LogNormal(1.5, 0.5) (mean and standard deviation of the log, in days) and isolation starts 2 days after onset on average:

julia
using EpiBranch
using Distributions
using StableRNGs

struct BorderClosure <: AbstractIntervention
    start_time::Float64
    leakage::Float64
end

function EpiBranch.competing_risk(bc::BorderClosure, parent, contact, state)
    parent.state[:region] == contact.state[:region] && return nothing
    return Risk(event_time = bc.start_time, block_probability = 1.0 - bc.leakage)
end

attrs = [
    clinical_presentation(incubation_period = LogNormal(1.5, 0.5)),
    (rng, ind) -> (ind.state[:region] = rand(rng, (:north, :south))),
]
iso = Isolation(onset_to_isolation_delay = Exponential(2.0), duration = 7.0)
process = BranchingProcess(NegBin(2.5, 0.16), Exponential(5.0))

isolation_only = simulate(ModelSpec(process; interventions = [iso], attributes = attrs),
    200; max_cases = 500, rng = StableRNG(42))
with_closure = simulate(
    ModelSpec(process; interventions = [iso, BorderClosure(10.0, 0.05)], attributes = attrs),
    200; max_cases = 500, rng = StableRNG(42))
(isolation_only = containment_probability(isolation_only),
 with_closure = containment_probability(with_closure))
(isolation_only = 0.85, with_closure = 0.905)

Adding the closure raises the proportion of outbreaks contained, because from day 10 most transmission between regions is blocked on top of what isolation prevents.

Who triggers contact tracing ​

ContactTracing decides which cases have their contacts traced through an eligibility policy, such as OnSymptomOnset() or OnLabConfirmation(), and policies combine with &, | and ! (see Isolation and contact tracing). A policy the built-ins cannot express is a type subtyping EpiBranch.TraceEligibility with one is_eligible method, which receives the infector, the contact and the simulation state. It then combines with the operators like a built-in policy.

Here only symptomatic infectors aged 65 or over have their contacts traced:

julia
using EpiBranch
using Distributions
using StableRNGs

struct SymptomaticOver65 <: EpiBranch.TraceEligibility end

function EpiBranch.is_eligible(::SymptomaticOver65, infector, contact, state)
    !EpiBranch.is_asymptomatic(infector) && get(infector.state, :age, 0) >= 65
end

attrs = [clinical_presentation(incubation_period = LogNormal(1.5, 0.5)),
    demographics(age_distribution = Normal(50, 20))]
iso = Isolation(onset_to_isolation_delay = Exponential(2.0), duration = 7.0)
process = BranchingProcess(NegBin(2.5, 0.16), Exponential(5.0))
n_traced(runs) = sum(s -> count(ind -> get(ind.state, :traced, false), s.individuals), runs)

everyone = ContactTracing(OnSymptomOnset(), 0.7, Exponential(1.0), Quarantine(duration = Inf))
older_only = ContactTracing(SymptomaticOver65(), 0.7, Exponential(1.0), Quarantine(duration = Inf))
traced_all = simulate(ModelSpec(process; interventions = [iso, everyone], attributes = attrs),
    100; max_cases = 200, rng = StableRNG(5))
traced_older = simulate(ModelSpec(process; interventions = [iso, older_only], attributes = attrs),
    100; max_cases = 200, rng = StableRNG(5))
(all_symptomatic = n_traced(traced_all), symptomatic_over_65 = n_traced(traced_older))
(all_symptomatic = 3791, symptomatic_over_65 = 1067)

With ages drawn from Normal(50, 20), about a fifth of infectors are 65 or over, so far fewer contacts are traced. Combined with a built-in policy, SymptomaticOver65() | OnLabConfirmation() traces the contacts of older symptomatic cases and of every laboratory-confirmed case.

Ending an infection early ​

A treatment that ends an infection before symptom onset, such as a post-exposure antiviral, calls EpiBranch.abort_infection! with the time the infection ends. On every transmission model the case then transmits nothing from that time, never develops symptoms (so it is never isolated on symptoms) and has no clinical transitions from that time on (see Individual state and reserved keys).

Here every exposed contact is treated and its infection ends delay days after exposure, unless symptoms would start first:

julia
struct Antiviral <: AbstractIntervention
    delay::Float64
end

function treat!(av::Antiviral, ind)
    isfinite(ind.infection_time) || return nothing   # not infected yet
    incubation = get(ind.state, :incubation_period, NaN)
    ends = ind.infection_time + av.delay
    if !isnan(incubation) && ends >= ind.infection_time + incubation
        return nothing    # symptoms start before the treatment would work
    end
    return EpiBranch.abort_infection!(ind, ends)
end

# Branching processes: each contact, at its potential exposure time.
function EpiBranch.trace_contacts!(av::Antiviral, state, infector, contacts)
    foreach(c -> treat!(av, c), contacts)
    return nothing
end

# Continuous-time models: each case once its infection time is fixed.
function EpiBranch.on_infection_settled!(av::Antiviral, ind, state, rng)
    return treat!(av, ind)
end

Two methods are needed because the two kinds of model reach a new case at different steps. A branching process passes each contact to trace_contacts! with its potential infection time, before the contact is known to be infected. An infection that turns out to start at or after the time the treatment ends it, or not at all, keeps no record of the treatment. The continuous-time models pass contacts to trace_contacts! before their infection time is drawn, and treat! therefore skips a person with no infection time; on_infection_settled! reaches each case there once its infection time is fixed.

Susceptibility and infectiousness are risks too ​

A person's own susceptibility and an infector's infectiousness are handled the same way as an intervention: as risks that can block a transmission. The simulation puts every transmission to the built-in risks and your interventions alike. competing_risk is the single way to block a transmission, whether the reason is a vaccine, a border closure or the person's own susceptibility.

Six built-in risks are always present:

  • EpiBranch.HostSusceptibility blocks with probability 1 - susceptibility of the contact. A contact with susceptibility 0.3 escapes 70% of exposures.

  • EpiBranch.InfectorInfectiousness blocks with probability 1 - infectiousness of the infector.

  • EpiBranch.InfectiousSource blocks every transmission from someone who is not infected, so a contact kept in the simulation without being infected (see Following up contacts who were not infected) can generate contacts without infecting them. Usually every active person is infected and it does nothing.

  • The end of an infectiousness window blocks a contact timed at or after the earliest until state of the infector's window, such as their death or recovery (see Infectiousness windows). A window with no until blocks nothing.

  • EpiBranch.AbortedInfection blocks every transmission an infector makes from its :infection_aborted_time, so an infection ended by EpiBranch.abort_infection! stays ended after the intervention that ended it stops being active.

  • EpiBranch.HostImmunity blocks every exposure of a contact who has already been infected, until susceptible_again_time is in the past. It does nothing for a model whose contacts_of never offers an already-infected person as a contact, which is every built-in model. A model that does gets a new, separately recorded infection episode (see Individual state and reserved keys) instead of a reinfection overwriting the earlier one.

A susceptibility or infectiousness of 1.0 blocks nothing, and these risks have no effect unless a population characteristic sets a value below one. You can add to them with a competing_risk of your own. They always apply, so a risk of your own should not apply susceptibility or infectiousness a second time.

HostSusceptibility and InfectorInfectiousness apply on branching processes. The continuous-time models apply the same two values as multipliers on the rate of transmission instead (see Continuous-time models: further details), so they do not decide them contact by contact. Of the remaining four, only AbortedInfection is resolved there: the end of an infectiousness window is enforced by the window closing, the race proposes only from cases that have settled and are infected by construction, and HostImmunity is not applied at all, because the race has nowhere to put a second episode. A risk of your own applies there as it does on a branching process.

Following up contacts who were not infected ​

By default only the people infected in a generation go on to the next one and generate their own contacts; a contact who was not infected goes no further. keep_active lets an intervention keep other contacts in the simulation. It is asked once per case, about the contacts that case reached: return the ids of those that should keep generating contacts, and the ones not infected are added to the next generation. On the network and household models it is asked during tracing instead, about which traced contacts the ring grows from.

This is needed for contact tracing beyond direct contacts. To reach the contacts of contacts, the simulation has to generate the contacts of an infected case's contacts even when those contacts were never infected. Keep them here, and the built-in InfectiousSource risk lets them generate contacts without infecting anyone. The contacts are not yet known to be infected when keep_active is asked, so here every one is kept, and those that are infected go on as cases anyway:

julia
struct KeepEveryContactActive <: AbstractIntervention end

function EpiBranch.keep_active(::KeepEveryContactActive, state, targets, is_new)
    [t.id for t in targets]
end

ContactTracing with depth > 1 is the built-in intervention that uses this: it keeps the uninfected members of a ring in the simulation for one generation fewer than the ring's depth (one more generation at depth = 2), so a second-level ring reaches the contacts of contacts that ring vaccination then targets.

Scheduling an intervention ​

Scheduled starts any intervention on a given day: Scheduled(iv; start_time = ...). Built-in interventions have no start-time field of their own. Scheduled does not hold back on_infection_settled!: an intervention that acts through that hook acts from the start of the outbreak even when scheduled.

For an intervention that acts on each case through resolve_individual! or trace_contacts! and defines its own intervention_time, Scheduled runs it whether or not the schedule is active at the case's or contact's own clock, then checks the schedule again at the time intervention_time reports and undoes the effect with reset! if that falls outside it. An index case, infected on day 0, is therefore still isolated by a policy starting on day 10 once their own isolation, after onset and a delay, falls on or after it (see Interventions that start or stop during the outbreak). An intervention that proposes actions (see Proposing actions of your own) is checked against each action's own date the same way. One with no intervention_time of its own is instead gated before it runs, exactly as Scheduled always gated every hook: its default, -Inf, leaves reset! nothing to undo once the hook has already acted.

To opt into the action's-own-time check, the intervention defines two methods:

  • EpiBranch.intervention_time(intervention, individual): the time at which the intervention acts on the person (for example the isolation time).

  • EpiBranch.reset!(intervention, individual): undo its effect on the person.

A simplified version of the pair Isolation defines:

julia
EpiBranch.intervention_time(::Isolation, ind::Individual) = isolation_time(ind)

function EpiBranch.reset!(::Isolation, ind::Individual)
    clear_isolated!(ind)
    return nothing
end

A user then schedules the intervention as any other:

julia
# The closure has no date of its own (day 0); Scheduled starts it on day 10.
Scheduled(BorderClosure(0.0, 0.05); start_time = 10.0)

The border closure's own start_time is compared with each transmission time. Scheduled instead switches the whole policy on when the simulation reaches day 10, which is how built-in interventions are started.

A trigger of your own ​

start_after opens a schedule on a condition instead of a date, and takes any subtype of AbstractTrigger. The built-in Infections and ReportedCases are two; a trigger of your own works without any change to Scheduled. It needs one method, and has two traits with defaults:

  • EpiBranch.is_triggered!(trigger, state) says whether the condition holds now. The same trigger is reused across the runs of simulate(model, n), so keep nothing on it between calls and work out any count from state each time. It is asked about 1.7 times per person, so a count that goes through the whole population on every call, as ReportedCases does, makes a run take time that grows with the square of the number of people. A count the simulation already keeps, as Infections reads state.cumulative_cases, costs nothing.

  • EpiBranch.trigger_can_lapse(trigger) says whether the condition, once true, can turn false again. The default is true, the safe choice. Return false for a condition that holds for good once met, as both built-in triggers do: neither the case count nor the number of reports already made can fall. The answer matters for fitting: a schedule that can close again gives up the isolation releases the likelihood would otherwise use.

  • EpiBranch.required_transitions(trigger) names the clinical transitions the model's progression must include for the trigger to work, () by default. Building a ModelSpec without one of them gives a warning naming the trigger, because without the transition that writes what the trigger reads the schedule never opens. ReportedCases names Reporting, except at n = 0, when the schedule opens before any report exists.

  • EpiBranch.required_fields(trigger) names the population characteristics the trigger reads from Individual.state, Symbol[] by default. Scheduled adds these to the fields it already requires of the intervention it wraps (see Requiring values on each person), so the simulation's startup check catches a model missing either.

Here the border closes once 20 cases have had symptom onset:

julia
struct TwentyOnsets <: EpiBranch.AbstractTrigger end

function EpiBranch.is_triggered!(::TwentyOnsets, state)
    return count(
        ind -> is_infected(ind) && onset_time(ind) <= state.max_infection_time,
        state.individuals
    ) >= 20
end
EpiBranch.required_fields(::TwentyOnsets) = [:onset_time]

Scheduled(BorderClosure(0.0, 0.05); start_after = TwentyOnsets())

TwentyOnsets keeps the default trigger_can_lapse: a person infected a second time draws a new onset, so the count can fall. It reads :onset_time, which clinical_presentation sets, and names it with required_fields: a model built without that attribute now stops at the start with an error naming it, instead of leaving the border open for the whole run with nothing to say why.

Capacity limits ​

CapacityConstrained limits how many actions an intervention can take per period, such as the doses available or the appointments a clinic can offer. Contact tracing itself cannot be limited this way. Your intervention defines EpiBranch.capacity_key(iv), the name of the true/false value recording that a person used the resource, and EpiBranch.capacity_time_key(iv), the name of the value recording when. This places usage in the period its date falls in, whichever intervention sharing the same capacity gave it. Ring, group and mass vaccination use their dose-label names.

Every proposed recipient is known before admission, including group members found by searching a whole group. See Intervention actions for how to propose actions and the rules on timing. An intervention still on the deprecated apply_post_transmission! proposes no dated action, so a dose it gives is placed by the admitting call's own clock instead; it can still use the capacity names, provided that method changes only the people it receives.

Requiring values on each person ​

If your intervention depends on values set by a population characteristic (such as :onset_time), declare them with EpiBranch.required_fields, and the simulation stops with a clear error at the start when they are missing:

julia
EpiBranch.required_fields(::MyIntervention) = [:onset_time, :asymptomatic]

A custom vaccination ​

A new vaccination differs from the built-in ones in whom it reaches and when. What a dose does once given (efficacy, severity_efficacy, delay_to_immunity, mode and dose_label) is described by a VaccineEffect, which every AbstractVaccination holds. A new vaccination type stores one and returns it from EpiBranch.vaccine_effect; the rest of the vaccination code reads these parameters only through that method. The new type then gets, without further code:

  • initialise_individual!, which records every person as unvaccinated (:vaccinated and :vaccination_time, named after the dose_label) unless a population characteristic already recorded a dose, such as one from an earlier campaign;

  • competing_risk, the reduced susceptibility described in AbstractVaccination;

  • the checks on dose schedules made when a ModelSpec is built, so a later RingVaccination can name its dose in requires_dose.

It adds an intervention_actions method choosing whom to vaccinate and when, returning one EpiBranch.dose_action(v, ind, time) for each person it will dose. That is the same action protocol the built-in vaccinations use, so Scheduled wraps your vaccination as it wraps a MassVaccination, and CapacityConstrained does once the type defines the two keys described in Capacity limits. The simulation puts the actions through those limits itself. A dose admitted this way writes the per-dose values listed in Individual state and reserved keys and draws efficacy, severity_efficacy and delay_to_immunity for that person, whether they were given as numbers, distributions or functions.

Here a campaign on day 10 reaches everyone aged 60 or over. kwargs... passes any further keywords on, as ... does in R:

julia
struct OlderAdultVaccination{V <: VaccineEffect, B} <: AbstractVaccination
    effect::V
    min_age::Int
    campaign_time::Float64
    booster_uptake::B
end

function OlderAdultVaccination(; min_age, campaign_time, booster_uptake = 0.0,
        kwargs...)
    OlderAdultVaccination(VaccineEffect(; kwargs...), min_age, campaign_time,
        booster_uptake)
end

EpiBranch.vaccine_effect(v::OlderAdultVaccination) = v.effect
EpiBranch.required_fields(::OlderAdultVaccination) = [:age]

function EpiBranch.intervention_actions(v::OlderAdultVaccination, state, candidates)
    actions = EpiBranch.InterventionAction[]
    for ind in candidates
        ind.state[:age] >= v.min_age || continue
        push!(actions, EpiBranch.dose_action(v, ind, v.campaign_time))
    end
    return actions
end

older = OlderAdultVaccination(min_age = 60, campaign_time = 10.0,
    efficacy = 0.8, delay_to_immunity = 14.0)
older_model = ModelSpec(BranchingProcess(NegBin(2.5, 0.16), Exponential(5.0));
    interventions = [older], attributes = demographics())
older_results = simulate(older_model, 50; max_cases = 200, rng = StableRNG(1))
n_people = sum(s -> length(s.individuals), older_results)
n_vaccinated = sum(s -> count(is_vaccinated, s.individuals), older_results)
(vaccinated = n_vaccinated, people = n_people)
(vaccinated = 1425, people = 4279)

Only contacts aged 60 or over are vaccinated, and the vaccinated make up a minority of the people in these outbreaks.

Passing the keywords on to VaccineEffect lets the constructor take the same effect keywords as the built-in vaccinations. A parameter describing what a dose does belongs in VaccineEffect, where every vaccination gains it at once; a parameter describing whom a dose reaches belongs on the new type.

An effect only your vaccination has is a field of the type (booster_uptake above). Its per-dose draw is recorded by a method of EpiBranch.record_effect_draws!, which every dose calls. RingVaccination records post_exposure_efficacy and onward_efficacy that way:

julia
_booster_uptake_key(label) = Symbol("booster_uptake_", label)

function EpiBranch.record_effect_draws!(v::OlderAdultVaccination, contact, label, rng)
    EpiBranch.store_draw!(v.booster_uptake, _booster_uptake_key, label, contact, rng)
    return nothing
end

For a number, store_draw! stores nothing and EpiBranch.dose_value reads the value from the vaccination itself; for a distribution or a function it stores the draw.

A custom effect mode ​

A vaccine is leaky (LeakyMode: every vaccinee's risk is reduced) or all-or-nothing (AllOrNothingMode: a proportion of vaccinees are fully protected and the rest not at all). A third kind subtypes AbstractEffectMode and has a method of EpiBranch.realised_efficacy, which turns the efficacy a dose was given into the value stored on the person. Nothing else in the vaccination code needs to change.

Here a "partial responder" mode gives a proportion efficacy of vaccinees full protection, as all-or-nothing does, but gives the rest a fixed lower level of leaky protection instead of none. The example records eight doses directly, with the same dose functions the recipe above uses:

julia
struct PartialResponseMode <: AbstractEffectMode
    non_responder_efficacy::Float64
end

function EpiBranch.realised_efficacy(mode::PartialResponseMode, eff, rng)
    rand(rng, Bernoulli(eff)) && return 1.0
    return mode.non_responder_efficacy
end

partial = RingVaccination(efficacy = 0.6, mode = PartialResponseMode(0.2))
draws = map(1:8) do i
    contact = Individual(id = i, parent_id = 0, infection_time = 10.0)
    EpiBranch.record_dose!(partial, contact, 0.0, StableRNG(i))
    vaccine_efficacy(contact)
end

Every stored value is 1.0 (a responder) or 0.2 (the protection given to a non-responder), unlike the 0.6 a leaky vaccine would store or the 0.0 an all-or-nothing vaccine gives a non-responder.

Intervention actions ​

An intervention proposes actions, and Scheduled and CapacityConstrained decide which go ahead. Each proposal names a person, a date and a function that records the effect. Scheduled checks the date and CapacityConstrained checks the budget, both before the effect is recorded.

Schedule delivery and retain its protection ​

Suppose an index case makes two contacts on day 20 (Dirac(2) always gives 2 contacts, Dirac(20.0) always a generation time of 20 days). Both contacts can be vaccinated on day 10, while the campaign runs:

julia
using EpiBranch, Distributions, Random

process = BranchingProcess(Dirac(2), Dirac(20.0))
vaccine = MassVaccination(efficacy = 1.0, eligibility_time = 10.0)
campaign = Scheduled(vaccine; start_time = 10.0, end_time = 10.0)
model = ModelSpec(process; interventions = [campaign])
state = simulate(model; max_generations = 1, rng = Xoshiro(42))

(cases = state.cumulative_cases,
 doses = count(is_vaccinated, state.individuals))
(cases = 1, doses = 2)

The result is one case and two doses: the index case remains infected, and both contacts are protected before their exposures on day 20. The campaign ends on day 10, which stops new doses; the protection already given lasts.

Now limit the campaign to one dose:

julia
limited = CapacityConstrained(campaign; budget_per_period = 1.0)
limited_model = ModelSpec(process; interventions = [limited])
limited_state = simulate(limited_model; max_generations = 1, rng = Xoshiro(42))

(cases = limited_state.cumulative_cases,
 doses = count(is_vaccinated, limited_state.individuals))
(cases = 2, doses = 1)

This gives two cases and one dose: only one of the two contacts is protected. Scheduled(CapacityConstrained(vaccine; budget_per_period = 1.0); start_time = 10.0, end_time = 10.0) gives the same result, because both orders check the proposed delivery date and count only doses that go ahead.

Capacity counts a dose against the budget of the day it is dated, not the clock of the case that proposes it. Here both contacts' doses are dated day 10, so they draw on the same budget whatever day the index case was processed on; period = 7.0 would charge the second week, [7, 14), not the first. The default period = Inf gives one budget for the whole simulation.

Proposing actions of your own ​

A clinic appointment can use the same scheduling and capacity limits. This example offers each new contact an appointment on a fixed date and records attendance only if the appointment goes ahead:

julia
using EpiBranch, Distributions, Random

struct ClinicAppointment <: EpiBranch.AbstractIntervention
    time::Float64
end

function record_attendance!(person, time, state)
    person.state[:attended] = true
    person.state[:appointment_time] = time
    return nothing
end

function EpiBranch.intervention_actions(visit::ClinicAppointment, state, candidates)
    [EpiBranch.InterventionAction(person, visit.time, record_attendance!)
     for person in candidates if !get(person.state, :attended, false)]
end

EpiBranch.capacity_key(::ClinicAppointment) = :attended
EpiBranch.capacity_time_key(::ClinicAppointment) = :appointment_time

appointments = CapacityConstrained(
    Scheduled(ClinicAppointment(10.0); start_time = 9.0, end_time = 11.0);
    budget_per_period = 1.0)
model = ModelSpec(BranchingProcess(Dirac(2), Dirac(20.0));
    interventions = [appointments])
state = simulate(model; max_generations = 1, rng = Xoshiro(42))

[person.state[:appointment_time] for person in state.individuals
 if get(person.state, :attended, false)]
1-element Vector{Float64}:
 10.0

The output is [10.0]. Two appointments are proposed and the budget allows one. record_attendance! records that the resource was used and when. Attending has no effect on transmission, and all three people are infected here.

With the appointment on day 12 instead, nobody attends, because the schedule ends on day 11. A proposal that Scheduled or CapacityConstrained turns down is considered again only if the intervention proposes it again; nothing keeps a queue of appointments. The rules for proposing, admitting and keeping effects are in Proposing, admitting and keeping actions.

Custom clinical transitions ​

A case's own progression (testing, reporting, admission, treatment, recovery or death) is described by clinical transitions, given as the progression of a ModelSpec; the Clinical transitions tutorial covers the built-in ones. A transition of your own subtypes AbstractClinicalTransition and uses the same hooks as an intervention, called once per case as it is settled: initialise_individual! to set defaults, then resolve_individual! to work out what happens to the case and when. A person who is never infected never has the transition's values set, so code reading them uses get with a default.

A milestone other transitions can use ​

Suppose a case is reported only after a positive test, and the reporting delay counts from the test, not from onset. A Testing transition records :test_time, and the built-in Reporting then starts from it with from = :test_time. The incubation period is LogNormal(1.5, 0.5) and the test follows onset after LogNormal(0.5, 0.3) days (mean and standard deviation of the log):

julia
using EpiBranch
using Distributions
using StableRNGs

clinical = clinical_presentation(incubation_period = LogNormal(1.5, 0.5))

struct Testing <: AbstractClinicalTransition
    delay::Distribution
    sensitivity::Float64
end

EpiBranch.required_fields(::Testing) = [:onset_time]

function EpiBranch.initialise_individual!(::Testing, ind, state)
    ind.state[:tested] = false
    ind.state[:test_time] = Inf
    return nothing
end

function EpiBranch.resolve_individual!(t::Testing, ind, state)
    ot = onset_time(ind)
    isnan(ot) && return nothing                        # no symptoms, no test
    rand(state.rng) < t.sensitivity || return nothing  # test misses the case
    ind.state[:tested] = true
    ind.state[:test_time] = ot + rand(state.rng, t.delay)
    return nothing
end

testing = Testing(LogNormal(0.5, 0.3), 0.9)
reporting_after_test = Reporting(delay = LogNormal(0.0, 0.2), from = :test_time)

model = ModelSpec(BranchingProcess(Poisson(2.0), Exponential(5.0));
    progression = [testing, reporting_after_test], attributes = clinical)
state = simulate(model; max_cases = 100, rng = StableRNG(42))

ind = state.individuals[end]
(onset = onset_time(ind), tested = ind.state[:test_time],
 reported = ind.state[:reporting_time])
(onset = 44.00251450982874, tested = 46.00066949630534, reported = 47.06291594224072)

For a tested case the report follows the test, which follows onset. Reporting skips a case whose :test_time is still Inf because it was never tested, in the same way as it skips an asymptomatic case when timed from onset. Transitions are worked out in the order of the progression list, so a transition that reads another's values must come after it.

Competing clinical outcomes ​

Death and Recovery end the case: they are terminal. Any transition for which is_terminal returns true, and which has a terminal_event(t, ind) method returning (time, label), takes part. After every transition is worked out, the earliest terminal event across the list becomes the case's outcome, recorded as :outcome (the label) and :outcome_time.

A third outcome, loss to follow-up, is another type with these methods. terminal_target names the state it ends in, so EpiBranch can check that an infectious period ends there (see Terminal transitions and the end of transmission):

julia
struct LostToFollowUp <: AbstractClinicalTransition
    delay::Distribution
    probability::Float64
end

EpiBranch.required_fields(::LostToFollowUp) = [:onset_time]
EpiBranch.is_terminal(::LostToFollowUp) = true
EpiBranch.terminal_target(::LostToFollowUp) = :lost

function EpiBranch.initialise_individual!(::LostToFollowUp, ind, state)
    ind.state[:lost_candidate_time] = Inf
    return nothing
end

function EpiBranch.resolve_individual!(t::LostToFollowUp, ind, state)
    ot = onset_time(ind)
    isnan(ot) && return nothing
    rand(state.rng) < t.probability || return nothing
    ind.state[:lost_candidate_time] = ot + rand(state.rng, t.delay)
    return nothing
end

function EpiBranch.terminal_event(::LostToFollowUp, ind)
    t = get(ind.state, :lost_candidate_time, Inf)
    return isfinite(t) ? (t, :lost) : nothing
end

progression = [
    Death(delay = LogNormal(2.5, 0.4), probability = 0.05),
    Recovery(delay = LogNormal(2.0, 0.4)),
    LostToFollowUp(LogNormal(1.5, 0.5), 0.1),
]
model = ModelSpec(BranchingProcess(Poisson(2.0), Exponential(5.0));
    progression = progression, attributes = clinical)
state = simulate(model; max_cases = 200, rng = StableRNG(42))

outcomes = [ind.state[:outcome] for ind in state.individuals if haskey(ind.state, :outcome)]
(died = count(==(:died), outcomes), recovered = count(==(:recovered), outcomes),
 lost = count(==(:lost), outcomes))
(died = 2, recovered = 272, lost = 24)

Recovery has no probability, and most symptomatic cases recover. Loss to follow-up, after about 4.5 days from onset (the median of LogNormal(1.5, 0.5)), tends to come before recovery, about 7.4 days, and death, about 12 days. The cases lost are mostly ones who would otherwise have recovered. The same pattern works for admission to intensive care, or outcomes that depend on treatment.

A transition that does not end the case ​

A transition that is not terminal leaves out is_terminal and terminal_event. It marks a point on the timeline that other transitions or observation can read. Here reported cases are treated with an antiviral with some probability:

julia
struct AntiviralTreatment <: AbstractClinicalTransition
    delay::Distribution
    probability::Float64
end

EpiBranch.required_fields(::AntiviralTreatment) = [:onset_time]

function EpiBranch.initialise_individual!(::AntiviralTreatment, ind, state)
    ind.state[:treated] = false
    ind.state[:treatment_time] = Inf
    return nothing
end

function EpiBranch.resolve_individual!(t::AntiviralTreatment, ind, state)
    ot = onset_time(ind)
    isnan(ot) && return nothing
    get(ind.state, :reported, false) || return nothing   # only reported cases
    rand(state.rng) < t.probability || return nothing
    ind.state[:treated] = true
    ind.state[:treatment_time] = ot + rand(state.rng, t.delay)
    return nothing
end

A later Death transition can read :treated in its probability, so that treated cases die less often:

julia
Death(
    delay = LogNormal(2.5, 0.4),
    probability = (rng, ind) -> ind.state[:treated] ? 0.02 : 0.08,
)

To let progression_loglik evaluate the clinical timeline, the transition needs a EpiBranch.transition_loglik method; without one, progression_loglik stops with an error. It reads back the values resolve_individual! wrote and returns 0.0 when the transition could not have happened:

julia
function EpiBranch.transition_loglik(t::AntiviralTreatment, ind)
    ot = onset_time(ind)
    isnan(ot) && return 0.0
    get(ind.state, :reported, false) || return 0.0
    treated = ind.state[:treated]
    time = treated ? ind.state[:treatment_time] : Inf
    occurred = treated && time <= outcome_time(ind)
    ll = EpiBranch.transition_term(
        t.probability, t.delay, ind, ot, occurred;
        censor = min(EpiBranch.infection_aborted_time(ind), outcome_time(ind))
    )
    occurred || return ll
    return ll + logpdf(t.delay, time - ot)
end

treated = ModelSpec(BranchingProcess(Poisson(2.0), Exponential(5.0));
    progression = [Reporting(delay = LogNormal(1.0, 0.3), probability = 0.7),
        AntiviralTreatment(LogNormal(0.5, 0.3), 0.6)],
    attributes = clinical)
treated_state = simulate(treated; max_cases = 200, rng = StableRNG(42))
progression_loglik(treated, treated_state)
-693.3667108817993

EpiBranch.transition_term gives the term for whether the transition happened. Reading t.probability directly would be wrong in two cases: a probability built by exclusive_probabilities, whose alternatives share one random draw, and a case whose infection ended early, before the transition could take effect. The likelihood of a custom transition explains both.

A treatment drawn after the case has died or recovered did not happen. The censor passed to transition_term above reads it that way, and the transition says so on the case too with a EpiBranch.censor_after_outcome! method, as the built-in Reporting and Hospitalisation do. The package calls it once the case's outcome is known:

julia
function EpiBranch.censor_after_outcome!(::AntiviralTreatment, ind)
    if ind.state[:treatment_time] > outcome_time(ind)
        ind.state[:treated] = false
        ind.state[:treatment_time] = Inf
    end
    return nothing
end
